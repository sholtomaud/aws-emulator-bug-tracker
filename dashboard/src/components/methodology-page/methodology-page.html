Methodology

How the numbers are calculated

Every number on the dashboard comes from one file, data.csv, which a GitHub Actions workflow rebuilds every day at 00:00 UTC from the public issue tracker of .

The data

Each row is a UTC day, and each column is a running total as of the end of that day:

open
Issues open on that day.
agent_closed
Issues closed so far by an AI agent: a GitHub Bot account, or a login containing agent, bot, emulator-ai or automation.
human_closed
Issues closed so far by anyone else.

Pull requests are not counted.

The Doomsday Clock

Hours to midnight = (1 − agent resolution share) × 24

The agent resolution share is the fraction of all closed issues that an AI agent closed. The clock runs over one day on a 24-hour dial. At 0% it reads 00:00, the start of the day, with a full 24 hours to go; every 1% of agent share moves it on about 14 minutes. At 100% it reaches 24:00, the midnight that ends the day, and human engineers have been replaced on this tracker.

  • Safe: more than 18 hours to midnight (before 06:00; agent share under 25%).
  • Elevated: 12 to 18 hours (06:00 to noon).
  • Severe: 4 to 12 hours (noon to 20:00).
  • Critical: 4 hours or less (after 20:00).

Agent velocity

The speedometer shows how many tickets AI agents close per day, averaged over the last seven days (or all the data, while there is less than a week of it). Two marks on the dial give it context:

  • Humans: tickets closed per day by people over the same window.
  • New issues: the arrival rate, the change in open plus closed issues per day.

The red redline starts at the arrival rate. Once the needle passes it, agents are closing tickets faster than they are filed and the backlog shrinks without human help.

Bug doom loop

Doom loop ratio = issues opened ÷ issues closed (last 7 days)

Issues opened are the change in open plus closed issues; issues closed count both agents and humans. Above 1× the backlog grows however hard everyone works. The meter uses a log scale from ⅛× to 8×, so halving and doubling look the same distance from break-even.

  • Burning down: under 0.9×, the backlog is shrinking.
  • Holding steady: 0.9× to 1.1×.
  • Falling behind: 1.1× to 2×.
  • Bug doom loop: over 2×, or issues arriving with none closed.

The projection divides today's open backlog by the net daily change: how long until it doubles, or until it is empty.

Agent time to clear backlog

Today's open issues divided by the agents' daily rate: how long agents alone would take to empty the tracker if no new issues arrived. It reads Never while agents close nothing.
